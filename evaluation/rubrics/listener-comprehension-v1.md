# Listener Comprehension Rubric v1

Use this rubric to compare a standard explanation with one adaptation based on an explicit, request-scoped preference. It is not a preference survey or an audio-quality rubric.

## Eligibility

- Use at least eight consenting listeners who did not create or review the episode.
- Randomly assign at least four listeners to the control and four to the personalized version.
- Give both groups the same topic, factual claims, comprehension prompts, playback conditions, and time limits.
- Store only a random study identifier, explicit requested preference, assigned condition, scores, and consent/deletion state. Do not commit names, contact details, recordings, or raw profiles.

## Procedure

1. Before playback, ask three open-ended questions covering the target mental model. Do not show answers.
2. Play either the control or personalized artifact once.
3. Ask three equivalent open-ended questions in different wording immediately after playback.
4. Ask two recall questions 24–72 hours later without replaying the episode.
5. Blind the scorer to condition. Record factual errors separately from style or preference fit.
6. Delete the participant-to-study-ID mapping after follow-up or withdrawal.

## Scoring

Score each immediate question from 0 to 2:

- **0:** incorrect, contradictory, or no relevant answer.
- **1:** partially correct but missing the causal relationship or boundary.
- **2:** correct mental model including the relevant cause, sequence, or limitation.

Immediate comprehension is 0–6. Learning gain is post-playback score minus pre-playback score. Score each delayed-recall question with the same 0–2 scale for a 0–4 recall score.

## Gates

Personalization passes this prototype gate only when:

- the personalized group median learning gain exceeds the control median by at least 1 point;
- the personalized group median delayed-recall score is not lower than control;
- no personalized artifact introduces a factual error or removes a material caveat;
- at least 75% of personalized listeners report that the explicit preference was followed; and
- withdrawals and missing follow-up responses are reported rather than silently excluded.

A result from fewer than four completed listeners per condition is **inconclusive**, not a pass. This small study is directional evidence and must not be presented as population-level statistical proof.
